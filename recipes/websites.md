When I ask for a recipe, search these first, in order, before looking anywhere
else.

## General

- [aniagotuje.pl](https://aniagotuje.pl) — Polish home cooking. Everything is in
  Polish, so ingredient names need translating carefully rather than guessing.
  Vegetarian dishes are filed under `dania bez mięsa`.

## Sweets for people with diabetes

Ranked by whether a diabetes organisation or a dietitian stands behind the
recipe, and whether every recipe carries **grams of carbohydrate per serving** —
which is the number that matters, and the one most "diabetic dessert" pages on
the open web leave out.

- [Diabetes Food Hub](https://diabetesfoodhub.org/recipes/dessert) — the
  American Diabetes Association's own recipe site, with a dessert section. Full
  nutrition per serving. The obvious first stop.
- [Diabetes UK recipe finder](https://www.diabetes.org.uk/living-with-diabetes/eating/recipes)
  — several hundred recipes, checked by their dietitians, showing calories,
  carbs and sugars per serving, and filterable by meal type and special diet, so
  vegetarian puddings can be picked out directly.
- [Diabetes Fonds — nagerechten](https://www.diabetesfonds.nl/recepten/nagerecht-recepten)
  — the Dutch diabetes charity, in Dutch, with nutritional values per portion.
  Worth preferring for anything I will actually shop for here, because the
  ingredients and brand names are the ones on Dutch shelves. Their
  [guide to low-carb puddings](https://www.diabetesfonds.nl/gezond-leven/gezond-eten/zo-maak-je-een-koolhydraatarm-toetje)
  is a good primer rather than a recipe.
- [Voedingscentrum](https://www.voedingscentrum.nl/diabetesrecepten) — the Dutch
  government-backed nutrition institute. Fewer sweet things, but sober and
  reliable, and the portion sizes are Dutch.

Treat general recipe sites' "diabetic dessert" collections as a last resort:
they are usually assembled for search traffic and often give no carbohydrate
figure at all. **Sugar-free is not the same as low-carb** — flour, fruit and
milk all still count, so a page that only promises "no added sugar" has not
answered the question.

To add a site, add a bullet: the link, then a short note on what it is good for
and what language it is in.
