## Ingredients (English)

- 2 green courgettes (650 g)
- Half a head of garlic
- 2 onions (250 g)
- 3 sweet peppers, different colours (600 g)
- 1 aubergine (400 g)
- 200 g button mushrooms
- 2 large tomatoes (650 g)
- 300 g bottled passata (sieved tomatoes)
- 10 tbsp olive oil
- Herbs: a handful each of oregano and basil
- Spices: 1 tbsp each of salt, sweet paprika and smoked paprika, plus 1 tsp pepper

## Method

1. Get a large pot and all the vegetables ready. Wash the vegetables well and lay
   them out on the worktop or in a bowl, so nothing gets forgotten along the way.
2. Peel the garlic and the onions. Slice the garlic cloves and chop the onions.
   Pour the oil into a heated pot or a large deep frying pan, add the onion and
   garlic, and fry over medium heat.
3. While they fry, cut the peppers: remove the seed cores and stalks and cut the
   flesh into pieces of about 2–3 cm.
4. Add the peppers to the softened onion and garlic. Stir with a wooden spoon and
   carry on frying over medium heat. Do not cover the pot.
5. Next go in the mushrooms and the aubergine. Small firm button mushrooms need no
   peeling — just quarter them; peel large ones and cut them into smaller pieces.
   Dice the aubergine without peeling it, trimming off both ends.
6. Stir again and keep frying, still uncovered.
7. Very young courgettes need only their ends trimmed and can be diced unpeeled;
   larger ones can be peeled thinly. Chop the herbs roughly.
8. Add the courgette and herbs, mix everything thoroughly, and cook over medium
   heat a little longer than before — about 10 minutes, still uncovered.
9. Peel the tomatoes thinly; blanching them first makes this easier. Chop them
   coarsely, removing only the tough bit where the stalk was. Beefsteak-type
   tomatoes are worth using, especially in summer.
10. Add the tomatoes with all their juice, pour in the passata, and add the
    spices: 1 tbsp salt, 1 tbsp sweet paprika, 1 tbsp smoked paprika and 1 tsp
    pepper. For a hot version, add a level teaspoon of chilli or cayenne.
11. Stir and cook until all the vegetables are soft but not falling apart —
    usually about 40 minutes in total from the moment the oil went into the pot.
12. Taste at the end. The quantity of garlic and paprika means no sugar is needed,
    though many recipes add it. Season further if it wants it.

## Substitutions and notes

- Nothing here is hard to find in the Netherlands. Use **passata** — `gezeefde
  tomaten`, sold in bottles or cartons — and not `tomatenpuree`, which is
  concentrate; the original is emphatic about the difference.
- **Gerookte paprikapoeder** (smoked paprika) is what gives this its character.
  Larger Albert Heijn and Jumbo branches carry it; a Turkish or Polish shop
  always does.
- The Polish original calls for `pomidory malinowe`, a pink beefsteak variety.
  The closest here is a `vleestomaat` or `coeur de boeuf`.
- Vegetable weights in the ingredient list are **before** peeling and trimming.
- A Polish `szklanka` (glass) is 250 ml, per the author's own note.
- Lecho is originally a Hungarian dish; this is the Polish version of it.
- The original suggests serving part of the batch with fried sausage stirred
  through. Left out here — the recipe itself contains no meat.
