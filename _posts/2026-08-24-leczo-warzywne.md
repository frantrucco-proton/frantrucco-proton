## Ingredients (2 people)

- 2 green courgettes (650 g)
- 3 sweet peppers in different colours (600 g)
- 1 aubergine (400 g)
- 200 g button mushrooms (champignons)
- 2 large tomatoes (650 g)
- 300 g bottled passata (gezeefde tomaten)
- 2 onions (250 g)
- half a head of garlic
- a handful each of oregano and basil

<!-- -->

- 10 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tbsp each of salt, sweet paprika and smoked paprika
- 1 tsp pepper

## Recipe

Prep 30 min · Cook 40 min · Makes 2.5 kg<br>
Per 100 g: 59 kcal · 6 g carbs (3 g sugars) · 2 g protein · 3 g fat<br>
Gluten-free, low-sugar, vegan, vegetarian
{: .recipe-facts }

Get a large pot and all the vegetables ready, washed and laid out, so nothing is
forgotten along the way. Peel the garlic and the onions, slice the garlic cloves
and chop the onions. Pour the oil into a heated pot or a large deep frying pan,
add the onion and garlic, and fry over medium heat.

While they soften, cut the peppers: seed cores and stalks out, the flesh into
pieces of about 2–3 cm. Add them, stir with a wooden spoon and carry on over
medium heat without a lid. Next go the mushrooms and the aubergine — small firm
button mushrooms need no peeling and can simply be quartered, larger ones are
worth peeling and cutting smaller, and the aubergine is diced unpeeled with both
ends trimmed off. Stir again and keep frying, still uncovered.

Very young courgettes need only their ends trimmed and can go in diced and
unpeeled; larger ones are better peeled thinly. Chop the herbs roughly, add them
along with the courgette, mix everything through and cook a little longer than
before — about 10 minutes, still without a lid.

Peel the tomatoes thinly, blanching them first if that makes it easier, and chop
them coarsely, removing only the tough bit where the stalk was; beefsteak
tomatoes are worth seeking out in summer. Add them with all their juice, pour in
the passata, and season with the salt, both paprikas and the pepper, plus a level
teaspoon of chilli or cayenne if you want it hot. Cook until every vegetable is
soft but not falling apart, usually about 40 minutes in total from the moment the
oil went into the pot. Taste at the end — the quantity of garlic and paprika
means no sugar is needed, whatever other recipes say.

## Notes

Buy **passata**, sold as `gezeefde tomaten` in bottles or cartons, not
`tomatenpuree`, which is concentrate; the original is emphatic about the
difference. Smoked paprika, `gerookte paprikapoeder`, is what gives this its
character and is worth a trip to a Turkish or Polish shop if the supermarket
lets you down. Vegetable weights are given before peeling and trimming. The
original suggests serving part of the batch with fried sausage stirred through;
left out here, as the recipe itself contains no meat.
