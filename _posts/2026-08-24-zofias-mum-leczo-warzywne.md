## Ingredients (2 people)

- 2 onions
- 3 cloves of garlic
- 1 carrot
- 2 sweet peppers in two colours
- 1 courgette
- 1 tin of chickpeas, 400 g (kikkererwten)
- a small bunch of dill (dille)

<!-- -->

- 2 tbsp oil
- herbs and spices, to taste

## Recipe

Total 15–20 min<br>
Vegan, vegetarian
{: .recipe-facts }

Cut the onion into half-rings rather than a fine dice, and slice the garlic.
Sweat them in oil in a frying pan until they turn translucent — *zeszklić* — which
takes only a moment. That is the end of the frying, and deliberately so: fry as
little as you can, and stew, boil or bake instead wherever the dish allows it.

Tip the pan into a saucepan and carry on there. In goes the carrot, sliced, then
the peppers in two colours, and those stew for a short while. The courgette comes
last, because it softens fastest and wants the least time of anything in the pot.
Put the lid on.

Add the chickpeas. Season with whatever herbs and spices you like, and finish
with dill scattered over the top.

The whole thing takes fifteen to twenty minutes, and it is meant to be short —
every vegetable in the pot could be eaten raw, so none of them needs more than
warming through and softening a little.

## Notes

The quantities are not part of the telling, which gives the order and the method
but no weights; the ones above are scaled down from the aniagotuje lecho for two
people, and none of them is exact. The chickpeas were offered as "for example",
so treat them as the swappable part.

It is a different dish from that lecho despite the shared name: no tomatoes,
passata, aubergine or mushrooms, carrot and chickpeas and dill instead, and
minutes of stewing rather than forty. One word is worth flagging — the oil is
the least distinct thing in the recording, so read *in oil* as the likeliest
reading rather than a certain one.
