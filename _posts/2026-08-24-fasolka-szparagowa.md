## Ingredients (2 people)

- 1 kg green or yellow wax beans (sperziebonen or wasbonen)
- 50 g breadcrumbs (paneermeel)
- 100 g butter (roomboter)

<!-- -->

- 2.5 litres water
- 1 tbsp salt

## Recipe

Prep 20 min · Cook 10 min · Makes 1 kg of beans<br>
Per 100 g: 103 kcal · 8 g carbs (3 g sugars) · 2 g protein · 7 g fat<br>
Low-sugar, vegetarian
{: .recipe-facts }

Tip the pods into a large colander in the sink and run cold water over them.
With a small knife, trim both ends off every bean, taking off as little as you
can so that water cannot get inside the pod while it cooks. This is the longest
part of the job, and as you cut the stalk end you will see whether a string pulls
away with it — if it does, the beans are no longer young.

Put the trimmed pods into a large wide pot with about 2.5 litres of cold tap
water; they will lift a little and may sit slightly proud of the surface. Cover
and set the heat high, and the moment the water boils add the salt and turn the
heat down so the beans only just simmer, still covered. Some cooks add a teaspoon
of sugar here to sharpen the flavour; the author does not.

Time the cooking from when the water came to the boil: yellow beans want 5 to 12
minutes, green ones 8 to 18. There is no single right answer, so fish one out
after a few minutes and taste it, and keep going if it needs it — but do not
overcook them, because overcooked beans swell, fall apart and taste worse. When
they are right, tip them back into the colander and rinse briefly under cold
running water to stop the cooking.

Now the breadcrumbs. Tip them into a dry frying pan, set the heat a little above
low and toast for a few minutes until golden brown, stirring constantly with a
wooden spoon. Once they are properly golden add the butter in small pieces, let
it melt and stir it through. Do it in that order: butter first would sit heating
and burning while the crumbs took longer to colour, or never coloured at all.
Tip the breadcrumb butter over the beans in a serving dish and turn them gently
so every pod is coated.

## Notes

The green ones are `sperziebonen`, sold everywhere all year. The yellow wax
variety is much harder to find — try a Turkish or Polish shop, or a market stall
in summer. Do not reach for `snijbonen`, which are sliced runner beans and a
different thing entirely. Young beans are the whole point: old ones are paler,
thick and long, with the seeds bulging under the skin, and snap into two halves
still joined by a woody string. A Polish `kostka masła` is a 200 g block, so the
original's half block is the 100 g above, and use real butter rather than a
spread. The nutrition figures are the author's own and cover the beans without
the breadcrumb butter.
