## Ingredients (English)

**For the beans**

- 1 kg green or yellow wax beans
- 2.5 litres water
- 1 tbsp salt

**For the breadcrumb butter**

- 50 g breadcrumbs (half a 250 ml glass)
- 100 g butter

## Ingredients (Dutch)

**Voor de bonen**

- 1 kg sperziebonen (groen) of wasbonen (geel)
- 2,5 liter water
- 1 el zout

**Voor de paneermeelboter**

- 50 g paneermeel (een half glas van 250 ml)
- 100 g roomboter

## Method

1. Wash the pods: tip them into a large colander set in the sink and run cold
   water over them.
2. With a small knife, trim both ends off every bean. Take off as little as you
   can, so water cannot get inside the pod while it cooks. This is the longest
   part of the job. As you cut the stalk end you will see whether a string pulls
   away with it — if it does, the beans are no longer young.
3. Put the trimmed pods into a large, wide pot and pour in about 2.5 litres of
   cold tap water. The beans will lift a little and may sit slightly proud of the
   surface.
4. Cover the pot and set the heat high. The moment the water boils, add the
   tablespoon of salt and turn the heat down so the beans only just simmer. Leave
   them covered. Some cooks add a teaspoon of sugar at this point to sharpen the
   flavour; the author does not.
5. Time the cooking from when the water came to the boil: **yellow beans 5–12
   minutes, green beans 8–18 minutes**. There is no single right answer, so after
   a few minutes fish one out and taste it, and keep going if it needs it. Do not
   overcook them — overcooked beans swell, fall apart and taste worse.
6. When they are right, tip them back into the colander in the sink and rinse
   briefly under cold running water to stop the cooking.
7. Now the breadcrumbs. Tip them into a dry frying pan. Set the heat a little
   above low and toast them for a few minutes until golden brown, stirring
   constantly with a wooden spoon.
8. Once the crumbs are properly golden, add the butter, cut into small pieces.
   Let it melt and stir it through the crumbs. Do it in this order: butter first
   would sit in the pan heating and burning while the crumbs took longer to
   colour, or never coloured at all.
9. Tip the breadcrumb butter over the beans in a serving dish and turn them
   gently so every pod is coated.

## Substitutions and notes

- Dutch shops sell the green ones as **sperziebonen**, everywhere, all year.
  The yellow wax variety is much harder to find here — try a Turkish or Polish
  shop, or a market stall in summer. Do not substitute `snijbonen`, which are
  sliced runner beans and a different thing entirely.
- **Young beans are the whole point.** Old ones are paler, thick and long, with
  the seeds bulging under the skin; snap one in half and the two halves stay
  joined by a woody string. The Polish season for young beans runs from May to
  the end of summer.
- `Bułka tarta` is **paneermeel**. The author is firm that quality matters:
  crumbs ground at home or bought from a bakery beat supermarket ones, and good
  crumbs do not scorch while toasting.
- A Polish `kostka masła` is a 200 g block, so the original's "half a block" is
  the 100 g given above. Use real butter — `roomboter`, not a spread.
- A Polish `szklanka` (glass) is 250 ml, per the author's own note.
- Other ways she serves the boiled beans: on their own, whole or chopped; under
  a sauce; under nothing but warm melted butter; or with a fried egg.
- The original also suggests stirring chopped cooked beans into meat sauces.
  Not repeated here — the recipe itself contains no meat, and needed no
  adapting to earn its place on this site.
