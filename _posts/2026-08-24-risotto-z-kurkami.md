## Ingredients (2 people)

- 500 g fresh chanterelles (cantharellen)
- 250 g Arborio risotto rice (risottorijst), dry weight
- 60 g grated Parmesan-style hard cheese, rennet-free (vegetarisch)
- a small handful of fresh thyme (tijm)
- a handful of flat-leaf parsley (platte peterselie)
- 150 g onion or shallots
- 3 cloves of garlic
- 100 ml dry white wine, or another 100 ml of stock

<!-- -->

- 40 g clarified butter and 40 g cold butter
- 900 ml vegetable stock
- 4 tbsp olive oil
- ½ tsp salt and ⅓ tsp pepper

## Recipe

Total 1 h 30 min · Makes over 2 kg<br>
Per 100 g: 186 kcal · 19 g carbs (2 g sugars) · 5 g protein · 10 g fat<br>
Gluten-free, low-sugar, vegetarian

Read the whole thing through first, because the stages come quickly once the pan
is hot. Clean the chanterelles of sand and bits of forest floor under cold
running water, working briskly so they do not sit soaking, rinsing them one at a
time and patting them dry with kitchen paper. Leave them whole and halve only the
very largest.

Set a large heavy-bottomed pan with high sides over medium heat, add the
clarified butter, and put in all the chanterelles at once along with the thyme
sprigs. After a moment season with the salt and pepper, then fry uncovered for
about 8 minutes, stirring every few minutes; the mushrooms should give up a good
deal of water in that time. Tip the whole contents of the pan into a bowl and
pull out the thyme.

Start the risotto in the same pan, unwashed. Add the olive oil and the finely
chopped onion or shallot and fry gently for about 5 minutes until translucent,
then crush in the garlic and give it one more minute over low heat. Add the rice
and pour in the wine straight away, stir, raise the heat to medium and fry for
about two minutes, uncovered.

Now add the hot stock a ladleful at a time, beginning with 200 ml and stirring;
once the rice has taken up most of it, add the next. Carry on until all 900 ml
has gone in, waiting each time for the rice to absorb most of the liquid and
soften. The whole process should take no more than 25 minutes.

When the rice is soft and the sauce lightly thickened, add the bowl of
chanterelles with every drop of their juices, stir through, drop the heat to its
lowest and warm for another minute. Take the pan off the heat, add the cold
butter in pieces and the grated cheese, and stir hard for about a minute until
both have melted in — this is the *mantecatura*, and cold butter is what makes
the emulsion come out glossy rather than greasy. Finish with the chopped parsley
and, if you like, a few tips pulled off the thyme, and serve straight away.

## Notes

Two things make this vegetarian rather than nearly so. The stock is vegetable
stock: the original offers `bulion drobiowy lub warzywny`, chicken or vegetable,
and this takes the vegetable option, which is the version the author labels
vegetarian herself. And the cheese has to be rennet-free — real Parmigiano
Reggiano is made with calf rennet, which she flags too, so look for
`Italiaanse harde kaas` or anything marked `vegetarisch`.

For a vegan version, she swaps the clarified butter for the same amount of olive
oil and the cheese for `edelgistvlokken`. Without wine, use 100 ml more stock
plus a tablespoon of lemon juice or wine vinegar, and taste for acidity at the
end. Frozen chanterelles work: thaw them on a plate on the worktop, tip them into
a sieve, pour boiling water over carefully and pat dry. The ingredient list
writes the salt as `0/5` of a teaspoon, plainly a slip for `0,5`, and the method
confirms half.
