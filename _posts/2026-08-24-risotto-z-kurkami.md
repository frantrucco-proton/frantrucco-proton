## Ingredients (English)

**For the chanterelles**

- 500 g fresh chanterelles
- 40 g clarified butter
- A small handful of fresh thyme sprigs
- ½ level tsp salt
- ⅓ level tsp pepper

**For the risotto**

- 250 g Arborio risotto rice (dry weight)
- 900 ml vegetable stock
- 100 ml dry white wine, or a further 100 ml stock
- About 150 g onion or shallots
- 3 cloves garlic
- 4 tbsp olive oil
- 60 g grated Parmesan-style hard cheese, rennet-free
- 40 g cold butter
- A handful of chopped flat-leaf parsley

## Method

1. Read the recipe through before starting — the stages come quickly once the
   pan is hot.
2. Clean the chanterelles of sand and bits of forest floor under cold running
   water, working briskly so they do not sit soaking. Rinse them one at a time,
   then pat dry gently with kitchen paper. Leave them whole; halve only the very
   largest.
3. Set a large heavy-bottomed pan with high sides over medium heat and add the
   40 g of clarified butter.
4. Add all the chanterelles at once, along with the thyme sprigs. After a moment
   add the ½ tsp salt and ⅓ tsp pepper. Fry over medium heat, uncovered, for
   about 8 minutes, stirring every few minutes. The mushrooms should give up a
   good deal of water in this time.
5. Tip the entire contents of the pan into a separate bowl and pull out the thyme
   sprigs.
6. Start the risotto in the same pan, unwashed. Add the 4 tbsp olive oil, then
   the finely chopped onion or shallot. Fry gently for about 5 minutes until
   translucent, then crush in the garlic and fry for one more minute over low
   heat.
7. Add the rice and pour in the wine straight away. Stir, raise the heat to
   medium, and fry for about two minutes, uncovered.
8. Now add the hot stock a ladleful at a time. Start with 200 ml, stirring; once
   the rice has taken up most of it, add the next. Carry on until all 900 ml has
   gone in, each time waiting for the rice to absorb most of the liquid and
   soften. The whole process should take no more than 25 minutes.
9. When the rice is soft and the sauce lightly thickened, add the bowl of
   chanterelles along with every drop of their juices. Stir through, drop the
   heat to its lowest and warm for another minute.
10. Take the pan off the heat. Add the cold butter, cut into pieces, and the
    grated cheese. Stir hard for about a minute until both have melted in — this
    is the *mantecatura*, and cold butter is what makes the emulsion come out
    glossy rather than greasy.
11. Finish with the chopped parsley and, if you like, a few tips pulled off the
    thyme. Serve straight away.

## Substitutions and notes

- **The stock is vegetable stock.** The original offers `bulion drobiowy lub
  warzywny` — chicken *or* vegetable — and this page takes the vegetable option,
  which is the version the author herself labels vegetarian. Use
  `groentebouillon`, and check the packet: some Dutch stock cubes still list
  animal fat.
- **The cheese has to be rennet-free.** Genuine Parmigiano Reggiano is made with
  calf rennet, so it is not vegetarian — the original says as much and tells you
  to check the label. In Dutch supermarkets look for `Italiaanse harde kaas` or
  anything marked `vegetarisch`; Albert Heijn and Jumbo both carry a vegetarian
  hard cheese that grates the same way.
- **Vegan version**, given by the author: vegetable stock, the clarified butter
  replaced by a similar amount of olive oil, and the cheese replaced with
  nutritional yeast flakes — `edelgistvlokken`, sold in any Dutch health-food
  shop and most supermarkets.
- **Without wine**, again from the author: use 100 ml more stock plus a
  tablespoon of lemon juice or wine vinegar. Alcohol-free white wine keeps the
  aroma. Taste at the end and consider a squeeze of lemon if the wine was left
  out entirely.
- **Frozen chanterelles** work: thaw them naturally on a plate on the worktop,
  tip them into a sieve, pour boiling water over carefully, then pat dry. Worth
  knowing here, since fresh `cantharellen` are seasonal and expensive in the
  Netherlands — a Polish shop or toko will have frozen ones year round.
- A tip from the author: drop a lightly crushed garlic clove into the clarified
  butter before the mushrooms go in, and lift it out once it turns golden and
  smells good.
- The ingredient list writes the salt as `0/5` of a level teaspoon, plainly a
  slip for `0,5`; the method says half a teaspoon, which is what is used here.
- The only time the original gives is `Czas przygotowania: 1 godzina 30 minut`,
  which covers the whole thing rather than prep alone.
