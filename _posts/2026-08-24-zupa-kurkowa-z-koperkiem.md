## Ingredients (English)

- 300 g chanterelles
- 300 g potatoes
- 1 small carrot
- 1 litre vegetable stock
- A small bunch of dill
- 2 cloves garlic
- 2 tbsp butter, clarified for preference
- ½ tsp salt and ½ tsp pepper
- 3 tbsp sour cream, 18% for preference

## Ingredients (Dutch)

- 300 g cantharellen
- 300 g aardappelen
- 1 kleine wortel
- 1 liter groentebouillon
- Een klein bosje dille
- 2 teentjes knoflook
- 2 el boter, bij voorkeur geklaarde boter
- ½ tl zout en ½ tl peper
- 3 el zure room, bij voorkeur 18%

## Method

1. Pour the vegetable stock into a pot and start heating it. Peel the washed
   potatoes, cut them into cubes and add them to the stock. Keep it cooking gently
   over a low flame.
2. Rinse the chanterelles well under cold running water and get rid of any grit.
   Halve the larger ones. Dry them on kitchen paper.
3. Heat a frying pan, then add the butter and the chanterelles. While they fry,
   peel the two garlic cloves, slice them, and add them to the mushrooms. After 10
   minutes of frying, season with the salt and pepper.
4. Tip the entire contents of the pan into the pot of stock.
5. Peel the carrot, grate it on the coarse side of a grater, and add it to the
   soup. Carry on cooking until the vegetables are soft.
6. Near the end, stir in the chopped dill.
7. Turn the heat down to its lowest. Put the sour cream in a small bowl, add a few
   spoonfuls of the hot soup and stir. Pour in another half glass (about 125 ml) of
   the stock and stir again. Pour this tempered cream into the soup and stir it
   through — passing it through a sieve as you go works well too.

## Substitutions and notes

- **Chanterelles** are `cantharellen` in Dutch. Fresh ones are seasonal — late
  summer into autumn — and expensive at that; a Polish shop or a toko will have
  frozen ones. The author says outright that the soup is just as good made with
  frozen chanterelles as with fresh.
- Do not let the name mislead you: `kurki` are chanterelles, despite the
  resemblance to `kura`, a hen.
- Polish `kwaśna śmietana` at 18% is a little lighter than Dutch **zure room**,
  which runs to about 20%. Zure room is the right thing to buy; `crème fraîche`
  at 30%+ is too rich here.
- Tempering the cream — warming it with a few spoons of hot soup before it goes
  into the pot — is what stops it splitting. Do not skip it.
- The stock is vegetable stock (`groentebouillon`), so check the packet: some
  Dutch stock cubes labelled `groentebouillon` still list animal fat.
